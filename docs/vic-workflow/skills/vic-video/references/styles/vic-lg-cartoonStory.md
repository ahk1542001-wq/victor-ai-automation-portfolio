# vic-lg-cartoonStory — VIC cartoon acting

Explain to an absolute tech beginner (roughly ages 10–15) without baby talk. Lead with a concrete task someone wants done, show what the agent and its tools actually do, then show the result and the human decision. Define a technical word when the story first needs it. A GitHub tool belongs in an agent use case, not a feature-list or installation-tour script.

## Direct a fresh story

Choose the characters, setting, palette, props and pacing for the new topic. The accepted agent-letter film is an acting/clarity reference, not a school-letter template. Preserve the original VIC face, outfit and proportions whenever VIC appears; do not substitute a vector redraw. VIC may read, explain, change shot size or leave the frame when the action is clearer without him.

Plan each beat with: what the viewer learns; the subject's intention; anticipation → action/contact → consequence/reaction; camera framing; what changes in the object; and the meaningful sound cue. Prop continuity matters: an object handed over should not leave an unexplained duplicate behind. Only genuinely held props follow a hand. Text panels stay in their own scene space. Cartoon facial reactions, articulated limbs, motivated squash/stretch and close-ups should carry the explanation; moving cards, arrows or a drifting background alone do not count as character acting.

Keep labels short and support them with visible action. Captions may preserve full spoken text, but do not repeat that narration as paragraph boards in the scene. Let the viewer see reading, selecting, writing, comparing or moving happen. Vary shot scale and action according to meaning rather than adding the same popup, zoom or effect to every scene.

Use motivated close-ups, pan/zoom and foreground/background parallax when they clarify an action; hold still for reading or a result. Keep captions and scene headings in screen space, independent of the world camera. Preserve prop contact, frame edges and text clearance during the whole move; hide an unnecessary actor in a detail shot rather than crop it into captions. This is layered 2D depth, not mesh-based 3D. The [camera/depth helper](../runtime-map.md) exposes applyWorldCamera; choose fresh per-shot camera poses and topic-specific depth props instead of copying the file-sort camera plan.

## Execute and bind

Use the reviewed local runtime at `<LOCAL_RUNTIME_PATH>`. Read [runtime commands](../runtime-map.md) for its entry points and protected regression fixture. A request for a video about file actions does not authorize actions on the owner’s real files; use newly created dummy data for any executable demonstration. Copy the [bundled rig](../runtime-map.md) beside the engine’s `core.ts`; it exports the character/shape helpers, caption layout and per-frame actor/text audit. Register only the assets used (`point`, `reading`, `blink`, `speaking`) and load the existing VICMyanmar font before rendering. Reuse this rig for character drawing only; keep the new film’s timing, captions and audio in its own modules. Do not import a prior job’s timeline or narration. Create a separate film/host entry and job outputs; preserve accepted masters and existing Hermes skills. Use deterministic frame-based animation. With the shared rig, begin and finish its text/actor bounds audit on every frame; place explanatory labels outside actor bounds, rather than disabling that check. Keep generated character assets versioned and inspect identity before use. Derive final shot lengths, caption ranges, mouth envelope and sound cues from measured narration; do not stretch speech to fit an estimated storyboard. After the shared voice driver has assembled verified output and stopped its owned runtime, use [bind_narration.py](../runtime-map.md) with `--work`, `--output`, `--module`, `--sound-import` and `--design-times`. Its generated module exports voiceTimes, voiceLevels, voiceCaptions, voiceFrames, voiceQuiet and narratedSound. The job-specific sound module exports `makeSound(sampleRate, duration, mapEventTime)`; the design-time JSON has one boundary more than narration chunks. This helper handles timing/mix binding only and grants no voice permission.

For cloned narration, read the canonical [shared voice procedure](../../../../docs/voice-and-colab.md) and use its existing driver and the actual job's upload/setup permission. Do not rewrite that driver, silently switch providers or reuse another job's listening-review receipt. Mouth amplitude animation is not phoneme alignment.

Compose sound for the new story: readable narration first, music with phrases, rests and an ending that support the story, material/action-specific SFX and intentional breaths. Keep voice, music and effects as separate stems so quiet source music is not attenuated again with the effects bus. For separate stems, export makeMusic and makeEffects with the same arguments as makeSound and run bind_narration.py with --separate-stems; it applies smoothed voice ducking to music separately. Measure the music stem as well as the final mix, then check audibility and character in playback; loudness measurements alone do not prove either. Do not force this film’s melody, instrument set or tempo onto future topics. Verify the final encoded mix rather than merely listing sound events.

## Review the encoded film

Check actual shots and transitions for causal clarity, character/prop contact, unexpected duplicates, static diagram stretches, original-avatar identity, caption legibility and overlaps. Inspect encoded boundary frames, not source screenshots alone. Compare the encoded export with source frames and run full decode, dimensions/frame-count/duration, audio sync, loudness and peak checks. Declare real intentional pauses honestly; do not hide defects by weakening thresholds. Mechanical PASS is not creative acceptance or proof of complete spoken words. State exactly what was visually inspected and listened to; keep owner acceptance and publication separate.
