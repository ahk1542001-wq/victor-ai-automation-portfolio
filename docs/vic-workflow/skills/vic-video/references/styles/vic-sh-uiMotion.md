## Sustained explanation and product evidence

Entrance is scene setup, not the entire explanation. For a multi-sentence scene, plan attention beats against measured spoken subpoints: establish the product/output, isolate the relevant feature, visibly perform the operation, show the changed result, then resolve/cut. Typical short beats may be a few seconds, but reading or a genuine demo may need longer; no compulsory cut interval or decorative movement quota. A long scene needs meaningful internal progression rather than one pop/slide plus a prolonged unchanged screenshot. Do not accelerate narration to fake pace.

Use a deliberate vocabulary as the content needs it: layered object assembly, cursor/selection and focused crops on authentic screens, transfer with visible receiver, before/after states, stepwise paths, highlighted result, motivated close-up/pan and match/action cuts. Verify each operation is actually supported and encoded; camera intent in a storyboard does not prove camera execution. Repeated floating, breathing, entrance loops or captions changing do not count as a process demonstration. A flattened diagram with all steps baked into one PNG is not animated explanation merely because the PNG slides in; separate its meaningful elements/states when the story needs them.

For product/GitHub stories, put actual product identity, interface, representative output and feature details on screen wherever they substantiate the narration. Use approved official screenshots/available authorized demo clips; capture a sequence or real interaction only when the task/tool/rights scope permits. A cropped still remains a still. Reveal a specific region at readable scale, combine comparison/detail/result views, and avoid a generic laptop/phone icon standing in for every feature. Do not meet an arbitrary screenshot count or add unrelated stock photos. Source/provenance/privacy/reuse checks stay in research-and-script. If useful footage is unavailable, disclose the limitation and propose a clear conceptual illustration; never invent UI or an executed demo.

Select a small coherent palette from product identity and the topic's mood. Cobalt is one option, not the next-film default. Keep background motifs subtle and relevant, with foreground contrast; change composition, scale and evidence focus more than background color. Official screenshots/logos keep their actual colors and aspect ratios. Background decoration cannot compensate for an inactive foreground.

Before proof, identify the longest unchanged foreground intervals and justify readable holds; shorten, subdivide or add meaningful state/detail beats where narration advances but visuals do not. Main reviews temporal intervals at real playback pace, especially a representative middle/late scene, not only attractive opening keyframes. Pair cues/music changes to actual events with readable settles; no effect on every noun. Use existing production flow small scene-group tasks and disjoint asset/sound preparation; main checks the continuity and pace across groups.
